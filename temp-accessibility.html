Accessibility

Last updated 24 August 2026Gausyn, a product of Inquisara

A personal teacher for every student is a claim with an obligation attached. A tutor that only some students can operate is not the product we set out to build.

1.Our commitment

We are building Gausyn to be usable by students with disabilities, including students who use screen readers, who navigate by keyboard, who need high contrast or larger text, who cannot use a mouse or stylus precisely, or who rely on captions.

We are not finished. This page says honestly where we are, because a vague accessibility statement helps nobody.

2.The standard we build to

We are working towards WCAG 2.1 Level AA as our baseline, which is also the standard referenced by the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) and by procurement rules at many schools and universities.

We have not yet completed a formal third-party audit or published a VPAT. Both are on the roadmap before Gausyn opens publicly, and institutions that need documentation to evaluate us should write to us in the meantime.

3.What works today

  • Voice as a first-class input. Every question can be asked out loud instead of typed, and every reply is spoken as well as written, which helps students with dyslexia, motor impairments, or visual impairments.
  • Text as a first-class input. The reverse is equally true: nothing in Gausyn requires a microphone. Deaf and hard-of-hearing students can run an entire session in text.
  • Reduced motion is respected. If your operating system is set to reduce motion, our animations do not play; scenes settle at their final, readable state rather than looping.
  • Pace is set by the student. Nothing advances on a timer. A lesson waits for you, and "slower" and "go back" are always available.
  • Zoom and reflow. Pages use relative sizing and reflow rather than fixed layouts, so browser zoom and larger system text sizes work.

4.Known gaps

We would rather list these than let you discover them:

  • The whiteboard canvas is inherently visual. Drawing requires a pointer, stylus, or finger, and its contents are not currently exposed to screen readers as structured text. A student who cannot draw can still work entirely by voice and text, but the board itself is not yet an accessible surface.
  • Diagrams generated by the tutor do not yet carry alternative text descriptions.
  • Uploaded worksheets are rendered as they were authored. If the original PDF is an inaccessible scan, Gausyn cannot make it accessible.
  • Keyboard navigation through board tabs and tool panels is incomplete.
  • We have not verified colour contrast across every state of every component.

5.Assistive technology

Gausyn is a web application and runs in current versions of Chrome, Safari, Edge, and Firefox. We test with the screen readers and magnifiers built into macOS, Windows, and iOS. If you use a tool we have not tested and something is broken, that is exactly the report we want.

6.Asking for an accommodation

If a barrier in Gausyn stops a student from learning, write to us and describe it. We will tell you honestly whether we can fix it, work around it, or not yet solve it, and roughly when. Where a student needs material in an alternate format to use Gausyn at all, ask and we will arrange it.

7.Feedback

Accessibility reports go straight to the people who can fix them:

Inquisara, makers of Gausyn
Toronto, Ontario, Canada
gausyninc@gmail.com

Tell us the page, what you were trying to do, and the assistive technology you were using. We aim to acknowledge accessibility reports within five business days.
