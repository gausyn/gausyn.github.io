From one board to four places to learn

The Gausyn team · October 2026 · 2 min read

The first version of Gausyn was one idea: a board you and your teacher share. It worked, and it also showed us what was missing. A student’s week is not all lessons. There are questions late at night, essays that take a month, and friends stuck on the same homework.

So with this release Gausyn grew from one board into four places to learn, with the whiteboard, where lessons happen, still at the heart of it.

Questions between sessions

Learning gives every subject its own teacher, there at any hour. Each one knows your level and your week, and when a question deserves more room, it opens a whiteboard session.

Lessons themselves got better too. They now draw each step right beside the words, and wait for the drawing and the voice before asking you anything. Every lesson gets its own tab, with its whole topic as the title.

The long work

Projects puts your essay, deck, or build on the right of the screen, written live as you watch. Slides are real slides, with ten themes, pictures drawn for the work, a Present mode, and export to PowerPoint, Word, or PDF.

Ask for an app or a game, 2D or 3D, and you can play it in the Preview as soon as its files appear. Engineering and hobby projects can design real parts in 3D, which you can turn, measure, resize, and download as an STL for a 3D printer.

Learning with friends

Collaborate is for study groups: shared boards in real time, named cursors, everyone’s handwriting in their own colour, voice and video, reactions, and a Friends room Gausyn never reads. Everyone has their own Gausyn on their own tab, and when a friend starts a lesson you get a note with a Join button.

The smaller things

Home now shows your latest conversations, your spaces and who is in them right now, and your newest projects. Settings moved into tabs, dark mode reaches every part of Gausyn, and a pass over the whole app made it better on a phone.

There are three plans now: Free to try Gausyn, Plus for a lesson most days of the week, and Pro for every day, exam weeks, and big projects. And if you ask Gausyn whether it is a real person, it says plainly that it is not: it is a computer program that teaches.
