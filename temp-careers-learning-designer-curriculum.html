1.About the role

gausyn teaches lessons out loud on a board, works through a student’s own worksheet with hints instead of answers, sets practice, marks timed tests, and talks through any subject with a teacher who knows it. Every one of those moments is a teaching decision, and you will make them.

You will read real sessions, find where the teaching failed the student, and change what gausyn does next time, for everyone.

2.What you will do

  • Write lesson sequences and paired practice worksheets across subjects and levels, from elementary school to university
  • Define what a good hint is, subject by subject, and how much help is too much
  • Decide how gausyn checks understanding in a lesson: when to ask, how long to wait, and what to do with a wrong answer
  • Write the guidance behind each subject’s teacher, so that a chemistry lesson sounds like a chemistry teacher
  • Build and organize the lesson and worksheet bank gausyn teaches from, by subject, grade and topic
  • Review real sessions, shared with permission, and mark where the teaching worked and where it failed
  • Shape how progress is measured: practice, timed tests, marking, and what gausyn remembers about each student
  • Work with engineers to turn your decisions into rules gausyn follows, then check that it follows them
  • Make sure explanations suit the student’s age, level and curriculum, in the US and in Canada
  • Set the standard for accuracy, inclusiveness and accessibility in everything gausyn teaches

3.What we are looking for

  • Several years of teaching real students, in a classroom or one to one
  • Lessons, curriculum or materials you are proud of and can show us
  • Deep knowledge of at least one subject, and a feel for where students get stuck in it
  • Opinions about why explanations fail, with examples
  • Experience writing assessments: practice, quizzes, tests and mark schemes
  • Comfort reading transcripts and giving specific, actionable feedback
  • Able to lead your own work with little guidance in a team that moves fast
  • Writes clearly and concisely, for students, parents and engineers alike
  • Excitement about teaching one student well, a million times

4.Nice to have

  • You have taught more than one subject or level
  • You have written materials other teachers use
  • You know a curriculum well, such as a US state standard or a Canadian provincial one
  • You have taught students with learning differences
  • You have worked closely with engineers, or on a product for learning
  • You have studied how people learn, and put it into practice
