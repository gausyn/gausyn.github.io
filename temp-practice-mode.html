The loop

Hints that land beside the problem, not on top of it.

This is the heart of Practice mode: you write, it watches, and when you stall it asks the one question that gets you moving — placed right next to the problem it belongs to. Every hint is a nudge or a single step, checked against the reference solution Gausyn worked out when the sheet loaded. The moment of actually getting it right is never taken from you.

It reads the page like a teacher

It knows where every question, blank, and option sits.

When your worksheet loads, Gausyn maps its geography: where each question starts, where the blanks are, where the multiple-choice options sit. So a fill-in gets its nudge beside the blank, a multiple-choice question gets one near the options, and a show-your-work problem gets a worked step only where there is room. One rule is absolute: it never writes in your answer spaces. The pen, and the moment of getting it right, are yours.

An infinite worksheet

Ask for practice, and it makes the questions itself.

Beyond the bank there is a second source of practice: Gausyn writes questions on the spot, one at a time, on any topic in your subject. Say “harder” and the next one climbs. “Easier” and it steps back down. “More like word problems” and the same skeleton puts on different clothes. Each question lands in its own Practice tab, pinned at the top of the board, and the Socratic loop carries on underneath it — hints, your handwriting, never the answer. It is a worksheet that never runs out and never stops listening.

Under the hood

Grounded before it says a word.

The moment your worksheet loads, two things happen that you never see. Gausyn works every problem out for itself, start to finish, and keeps those reference solutions beside it — so when it checks your third line, it is checking against real arithmetic, not a guess. And it maps the page itself, so every piece of help it ever gives has a correct place to stand.

It solves your sheet first

Before the first hint, Gausyn has already solved every numbered problem on your worksheet, step by step. Those solutions ground everything it says afterwards — when it tells you a line checks out, that is a fact, not a vibe.

Questions, blanks, and options are located on the page — so hints stand exactly where they should.

It reads the page’s geography

Typed or scanned, the sheet is mapped: question lines, underscore blanks, answer boxes, choice options. Hint placement is deterministic — beside the blank, under the question, near the options — never scattered wherever a model felt like pointing.

In full

Everything Practice mode does.

01

Bring anything, or bring nothing

Practise on the worksheet paired with your lesson, pull a different sheet from the bank — “get me a worksheet on trig identities” — upload tonight’s actual homework as a PDF or photos, or ask Gausyn to generate questions from nothing. All four flow through the same board, the same conversation, the same rules of help.

02

A bank written by teachers

The worksheet bank pairs every lesson with practice written to match it — real teachers’ material, matched to your session by subject, grade, and topic, millions of problems deep and growing. This is practice with a point of view, not a random question generator wearing a worksheet costume.

03

It solves the sheet before you start

On upload, Gausyn works every problem to a full reference solution and keeps it private. Your work is checked against real worked math for the rest of the session — which is why it can say “that line is right, keep going” with a straight face, and catch the exact step where things went sideways.

04

It sees your handwriting

Every exchange, Gausyn looks at the board as it actually is — your strokes, your crossed-out attempts, your half-finished line. Help responds to what you actually wrote, not to what a transcript claims you meant. Write it the way you would on paper; it reads it the way a teacher leaning over your shoulder would.

05

Hints, never answers

The help is Socratic by design. A nudge in plain words. One step of math, typeset properly. A question that makes you notice what you missed. Gausyn is graded on whether you could do the next one alone — so it refuses to do this one for you, even when you ask nicely.

06

Three kinds of help, one placement engine

Plain words, a formula step, or a drawn diagram — whichever the moment needs. All three land by the same deterministic rules: stacked beside the question they belong to, out of the way of your ink and the worksheet text, pruning the oldest notes when space runs short. The board stays legible for the whole session.

07

It draws when a picture teaches better

Ask “can you draw this?” — or let it judge that words are failing — and a labelled diagram is sketched beside the problem: the triangle in the word problem, the forces on the block, the shape of the graph. Drag it, resize it, keep it for the rest of the session.

08

Infinite practice that tunes itself

Generated questions arrive one at a time in their own Practice tab, pinned where you cannot lose them. “Harder,” “easier,” “a different one,” “more like word problems” — the next question adjusts instantly, even mid-problem. Breadth when you want variety, depth when you want a fight, mercy when you need a win.

09

Your blanks stay yours

Gausyn never writes in an answer space — not a number, not a letter, not helpfully, not once. It can annotate the margin and circle what you are confirming, but the answer is always in your handwriting. That is the difference between finishing the homework and being changed by it.

10

Boards that remember, and room when you need it

Every board tab keeps its ink, its PDF, its scroll position, and every hint exactly where it was — switch away and back and nothing has moved. And when one problem deserves a full page of thinking, drag a box around it and Ask Gausyn opens a workspace just for it.

Practice makes permanent. Make it count.
