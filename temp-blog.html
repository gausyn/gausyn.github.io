What is new in Gausyn.

Every time we release a new version of Gausyn, we write down what changed for you in plain language. The newest version is at the top.

Version 0.1 August 2026 · First release

The first version of Gausyn that students can sit down and use. It covers the whole loop: planning your week, being taught a lesson, and working through problems on a shared board.

Whiteboard

A board you and your teacher share

Write with a pen, drop in shapes, or upload a worksheet and work straight on top of it. Gausyn sees the page the same way you do, so it can talk about the actual question in front of you instead of a description of it.

Teacher mode

Lessons taught out loud

Pick a topic and Gausyn teaches it page by page, speaking as it goes. Interrupt at any point to ask why, ask for it slower, or ask it to go back. It waits for you rather than running on a timer.

Practice mode

Work the problem yourself

Bring your own worksheet or a photo of your homework. You do the writing; Gausyn watches, and when you stall it offers a nudge or a single step, placed right beside the question you are stuck on. It will not fill in your answers.

Hints

Help that appears where you are looking

Gausyn works out every problem on the sheet for itself the moment you upload it, so when it checks your line four it is checking against real work. Hints stack next to the relevant question rather than scrolling away in a chat window.

Ask Gausyn

More space for a hard question

Drag a box around any problem and it opens in its own workspace, with the original pinned at the top and a clean grid underneath. A marker takes you back to where you were.

Practice questions

Ask for another one

Say you want more practice and Gausyn writes a fresh question on the spot, one at a time. Ask for harder, easier, or just different, and the next one adjusts.

Calendar

A week planned by talking

Tell Gausyn about an exam or an assignment in plain words and it books the study sessions around the hours you are actually free. Change your mind and it re-plans. You can still drag anything by hand.

Deadlines

Exams and due dates you can see

Add a test or an assignment and it sits on the calendar with a live countdown. Gausyn plans the week around it rather than up to it, and leaves the morning of the exam clear.

Sessions

Lessons that are real appointments

A booked lesson opens ten minutes early, runs on a clock kept on our servers, and finishes with a short note about what you covered. Reloading the page or switching devices does not change the time you have left.

Voice

Talk to it, or type

Speak your question and Gausyn answers out loud, or turn the microphone off and type instead. Everything works either way, so a quiet library and a noisy kitchen both work.

Levels

Grade one through graduate school

The same account covers early arithmetic, high school algebra and chemistry, university calculus, and graduate work. The explanations, pace, and vocabulary change with the level rather than the account.

Your files

We do not keep your homework

When you upload a worksheet, Gausyn reads it, writes down a short summary of the topic, and discards the file itself. The summary is what helps it prepare for your next session.

Have something you want Gausyn to do that it cannot do yet? Tell us, and it may well show up in the next version: get in touch.
