Written work

A draft that improves every time you talk.

Gausyn asks one or two questions before it assumes anything. Then the draft appears beside the conversation, always the complete current version. Push on a weak section and it comes back stronger. It never invents a quote, a statistic, or a source: when a claim needs one, it marks the gap and tells you where to look.

Build it in steps you can test.

For builds, Gausyn breaks the work into milestones you can check as you go: parts and tools first, then one testable step at a time. When the draft contains code, it runs right in the page, safely sandboxed, so you see the result before you ask the next question.

Six kinds of work

Whatever you are making.

01

Course project

Written coursework, planned and polished: outlines, drafts, tightened arguments, and prose that sounds like you at your best.

02

Presentation

An outline first, then slide by slide, then speaker notes, with the audience and the clock always in mind.

03

Paper or essay

Thesis, structure, evidence. Never a fabricated citation, and always a pointer to where the real one lives.

04

Engineering & code

Algorithms, circuits, robots, websites. Testable milestones, code that runs in the page, and safety first.

05

Hobby build

Practical and encouraging, budget-minded, free tools first, and a cheaper substitute for almost everything.

06

Anything else

Research, planning, practice, or something no category fits. Gausyn works out what kind of help it needs.

In full

Everything Projects does.

01

One home for every project

Each project keeps its conversation, its living draft, and its files together. Come back a week later and every decision you made is still there, so the work builds instead of starting over.

02

It asks before it assumes

When the scope is vague, Gausyn asks one or two sharp questions first: one cause or several, which sources are allowed, how long the talk runs. The first draft is built for your assignment, not a generic one.

03

A living draft, always complete

The draft sits beside the conversation, and every revision is the whole current version, never a patchwork of edits. Push on a weak paragraph and watch the draft come back stronger.

04

Your project, not ours

Gausyn coaches, drafts, and improves, and it keeps you doing the thinking. Write a section yourself and it will push on the argument, not quietly rewrite your sentences.

05

Honest about sources

It never invents a quote, a statistic, or a citation. When a claim needs support, it marks the gap and tells you where to look, so what you hand in is true.

06

Pitched to your level and your style

Vocabulary, depth, and rigor match your grade, and help matches how you like to work: walked through step by step, nudged with hints, or challenged with the minimum of hand-holding.

07

Code that runs in the page

For engineering projects, code in the draft runs right beside it: web pages render, JavaScript and Python execute, all in a sealed sandbox with no network access, so you see the result before you ask the next question.

08

Export when it is ready

Download essays and reports as Word or PDF, and presentations as PowerPoint or PDF, formatted and ready to hand in or present.

09

Your files, kept with the work

Upload the brief, the rubric, your earlier drafts, or anything the project needs, and they stay with the project for as long as it does.

10

Safe by design

Projects helps with legitimate work only. It declines dangerous builds and offers a safe alternative, and it will not help anyone cheat on a live exam.
