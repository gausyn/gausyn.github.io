Written work

A page that improves every time you talk.

Essays and reports render as a real page in the project’s theme. Gausyn asks one or two questions before it assumes anything, then writes the complete draft beside the chat. Push on a weak section and that section is rewritten in place, highlighted while it changes and glowing when it is done. It never invents a quote, a statistic, or a source: when a claim needs one, it marks the gap.

Causes of the First World War

Thesis

The war was not caused by one assassination, but by alliances, militarism, and imperial rivalry that turned a regional crisis into a continental war.

Outline

  1. The alliance system: two blocs that turned every dispute into a shared one.
  1. The arms race: military buildup across Europe.
    The arms race: after HMS Dreadnought launched in 1906, Britain and Germany raced to build battleships, and each read the other’s buildup as a threat. [figure needed: naval spending, 1906 to 1914]
  1. Imperial rivalry: competition for colonies and prestige.
  1. July 1914: from Sarajevo to mobilization in five weeks.

Primary source

The “Willy and Nicky” telegrams between the Kaiser and the Tsar, July 1914.

Kinds of work

Whatever you are making.

Pick a kind and Gausyn shapes its help around it. If none fits, choose Other and it works out what you need.

01

App or game

2D and 3D games, apps, and tools, written as real files and running live in the Preview as Gausyn builds them.

02

Presentation

Real slides written live: title, section, bullet, picture, and quote layouts, speaker notes under each, a theme, and Present mode.

03

Paper or essay

A themed page with a thesis, a structure, and evidence. Never a fabricated citation, and always a pointer to where the real one lives.

04

Course project

Written coursework, planned and polished: outlines, drafts, tightened arguments, and prose that sounds like you at your best.

05

Engineering & code

Parts in 3D you can print, circuits, robots, and programs. Testable milestones, code that runs, and safety first.

06

Hobby build

Plans, steps, and 3D models for something you make. Budget-minded, free tools first, and a cheaper substitute for almost everything.

In full

Everything Projects does.

01

A live viewer beside the chat

The work sits on the right, large, about sixty percent of the screen by default. Drag the divider to make it bigger, and it remembers where you left it, or press Full width to give it the whole screen. On a phone, Chat and Slides are one tap apart.

02

Written live, in front of you

Every new version streams in over the old one. The slide or paragraph being written is outlined, what is still to come waits faded, and the view follows the writing. When a version lands, whatever changed glows once and scrolls into view.

03

Real slides, not a list

Presentations render as true 16:9 slides, with title, section, bullet, picture, and quote layouts that scale with the pane and speaker notes under each one. One idea per slide; the depth lives in your notes.

04

Ten looks, or your own

Gausyn, Midnight, Ocean, Sunset, Forest, Lavender, Chalkboard, Bold, Minimal, and Candy. Change the accent, the background, or the font from the Theme button, or ask in plain words: “make it dark blue.” The look is saved with the project.

05

Pictures, drawn for the work

Where a picture genuinely helps, a title slide, a figure, something easier to see than to read, Gausyn draws one for your project inside a school-safe frame. Pictures start while the rest is still being written, and any of them can be redrawn with one click.

06

Present from the page

Press Present and the deck goes full screen. Arrow keys or a click move you forward, and you can start from any slide.

07

Export exactly what you see

Decks download as PowerPoint or PDF, documents as Word or PDF, straight from the draft, with the theme and the pictures in place.

08

It asks before it assumes

When the scope is vague, Gausyn asks one or two sharp questions first: one cause or several, which sources are allowed, how long the talk runs. The first draft is built for your assignment, not a generic one.

09

Your project, not ours

Gausyn coaches, drafts, and improves, and it keeps you doing the thinking. Write a section yourself and it will push on the argument, not quietly rewrite your sentences.

10

Honest about sources

It never invents a quote, a statistic, or a citation. When a claim needs support, it marks the gap and tells you where to look, so what you hand in is true.

11

Code that runs in the page

For engineering projects, a preview sits right under the draft: web pages render, JavaScript and Python execute, all in a sealed sandbox with no network access, so you see the result before you ask the next question.

12

Apps and games that run live

Games and apps are written as real files, with the code view following along as each one is written. The finished build runs in the Preview, 2D or 3D, with a console underneath, and one button sends any error straight back to be fixed. Download every file as a .zip.

13

3D parts you can print

Gausyn designs real parts in 3D. Turn and zoom the model, see its size in millimetres, drag its measurements as sliders, press Keep these to save them into the design, and download an STL for a 3D printer.

14

Effort, your call

An Effort switch sits beside the chat box: Low for quick changes and questions, Medium for most work, and High for big builds, tricky bugs, and detailed 3D, when you want Gausyn to think much longer.

15

No name needed

Start with nothing but the kind of project. Gausyn names it from the first thing you ask for, so a quick idea never waits on a title.

16

Undo, any time

Go back to the version before Gausyn’s last change, for drafts and code alike.

17

Your files, kept with the work

Upload the brief, the rubric, your earlier drafts, or anything the project needs, and they stay with the project for as long as it does.

18

Safe by design

Projects helps with legitimate work only. It declines dangerous builds and offers a safe alternative, and it will not help anyone cheat on a live exam.
