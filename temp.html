Why this needs to exist

The way we learn has not kept up.

Everything a student reaches for today was built to broadcast, not to teach. Lessons that cannot hear you. Courses tuned to an average student who does not exist. Apps that finish the homework and end the thinking. Gausyn exists to close the loop every one of them leaves open: the moment you say wait, I don’t get it.

Recorded lessons play at you

The video cannot hear you. Pause it a hundred times and it says the same thing, the same way, at the same speed. The moment you stop following, you are on your own.

One pace for a million students

Fixed curricula are tuned to the middle of an imaginary class. Nobody lives there. Real understanding slows down, doubles back, then suddenly sprints, and the course should move with it.

Lesson · Why a Chord Resolves
Lesson · page 3 of 6 Waiting for you

The chord that wants to go home

  • Build a chord on the fifth note of C major and you get G, B, D. Add F and it becomes G7.
  • B sits a half step under C and leans up. F sits a half step over E and leans down.
  • Together, B and F span a tritone, the interval that makes G7 sound unsettled.
G7 → C  ·  V7 → I
G, the root B, the leading tone D, the fifth F, the seventh
6 half steps, 3 whole tones: a tritone
Lesson · page 4 of 6

Where the pull goes

  • B rises a half step to C.
  • F falls a half step to E.
  • The tritone closes into a major third, C and E, and the tension is gone.
B–F, 6 half steps → C–E, 4 half steps
B up to C, a half step F down to E, a half step B to F was a tritone. C to E is a major third, and it rests.

Why do quakes cluster?Practice for Friday
Why do earthquakes keep happening in the same places?
Lena is thinking
L

Because rock breaks where it is pushed or pulled hardest, and most of that happens where plates meet.

Earth’s outer shell is cracked into seven large plates and many smaller ones. They creep a few centimeters a year, about as fast as your fingernails grow.

Along their edges the rock locks, strains for years, then slips all at once. That sudden slip is an earthquake.

Quick check: is Iceland in the middle of a plate, or on an edge?

In the middle? It is out in the ocean.
Lena is thinking
L

Not quite, and it is a common slip: the ocean hides its edges. Picture the long ridge down the middle of the Atlantic. Try once more?

So it sits on an edge, where two plates pull apart?
