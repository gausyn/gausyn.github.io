What a rate actually measures

  • How much of something appears or disappears, per unit of time.
  • Always a change divided by an interval, never a single reading.
  • The sign tells you which side of the arrow you are standing on.
rate = Δ[A] / Δt

Why concentration changes the rate

  • More particles in the same space collide more often.
  • More collisions means more of them clear the energy barrier.
  • Second order in A: doubling [A] quadruples the rate.
rate = k[A]²[B]

01

Speech only, on purpose

During a walkthrough the toolbar disappears. There is one thing to do — listen, and interrupt — so that is all the screen offers. The lesson has your full attention because it asks for nothing else.

02

It never advances on a timer

Every page turn waits for you. Say “go back”, “slower”, or “why?” in the middle of a sentence and the lesson reshapes itself around where you actually are, not where a syllabus says you should be.

03

Interruptions are the lesson

The question you blurt out mid-page is the most honest signal a teacher ever gets. Gausyn treats it that way: it stops, draws the answer if a picture helps, checks it landed, then carries on.

04

One sentence to switch

Say “let's practise” and the paired worksheet loads, the tools come back, and the same conversation continues into Practice mode. Say “switch to homework” and bring your own instead.

The best lectures were always conversations.
