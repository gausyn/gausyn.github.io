Lesson · Reaction Rates
Lesson · page 1 of 6

What a rate actually measures

  • How much of something appears or disappears, per unit of time.
  • Always a change divided by an interval, never a single reading.
  • The sign tells you which side of the arrow you are standing on.
rate = Δ[A] / Δt
Lesson · page 2 of 6

Why concentration changes the rate

  • More particles in the same space collide more often.
  • More collisions means more of them clear the energy barrier.
  • Second order in A: doubling [A] quadruples the rate.
rate = k[A]²[B]

Lesson · Projectile Motion
Lesson · page 3 of 7

Why projectiles curve

  • Gravity only ever pulls straight down, nothing pulls sideways.
  • So the horizontal speed never changes; only the vertical one does.
  • A steady sideways drift plus a growing fall traces a parabola.
Lesson · page 4 of 7

Splitting the launch velocity

  • Every launch hides two motions: one sideways, one up.
  • The angle decides how the speed is shared between them.
  • Treat them separately and every projectile problem opens.
vₓ = v cos θ    vᵤ = v sin θ

What a fraction actually says

  • The bottom number names the size of the pieces.
  • The top number just counts how many you have.
  • Same whole, different cuts, the bar never changes length.
3/8 of the bar
Grade 9 · Mathematics
Lesson · page 3 of 6

Keeping an equation balanced

  • An equals sign is a promise that both sides weigh the same.
  • Whatever you do to one side, do to the other, or the promise breaks.
  • Undo the last thing that happened to x, first.
3x − 7 = 14  →  3x = 21  →  x = 7
Grade 10 · English
Lesson · page 1 of 4

What a thesis has to do

  • It makes a claim someone reasonable could disagree with.
  • It says why, not just what, the because is the argument.
  • If your whole essay could not fail to prove it, it is a summary.
Weak: Macbeth is about ambition.
Arguable: Macbeth’s ambition only becomes fatal once it is witnessed, he kills to be seen as king, not to be one.
Grade 12 · Calculus
Lesson · page 2 of 8

What a derivative measures

  • The slope of the curve at a single instant, not over a stretch.
  • Take the average rate, then shrink the interval to nothing.
  • Whatever the average settles on as h vanishes is the derivative.
f′(x) = limh→0 [ f(x+h) − f(x) ] / h
University · Linear algebra
Lesson · page 5 of 9

Eigenvectors: the directions that survive

  • A matrix knocks almost every vector off the line it was on.
  • A few directions are only stretched or squashed, never turned.
  • Those are the eigenvectors; the amount of stretch is λ.
A v = λ v

In full

Everything Teacher mode does.

01

Planned by your week, not picked from a menu

Sessions come off your calendar with a subject, grade, and topic already attached, because Gausyn planned them around your real deadlines. An exam on Friday becomes rates on Tuesday, equilibrium on Wednesday, review on Thursday. When you join, the right lesson is already loaded. There is nothing to search, nothing to configure, nothing between sitting down and learning.

02

Real lessons, from a bank built by teachers

Lessons are not generated slideshows. They come from a bank of lesson and worksheet pairs written by real teachers, millions of problems across subjects and grades, each lesson matched to your session by subject, grade level, and topic, and each one paired with the practice worksheet written to go with it. Where the bank has no page for your corner of the syllabus, Gausyn generates the material itself, so no topic is ever out of reach.

03

Taught out loud, page by page

Gausyn narrates the lesson the way a teacher works a room: page by page, pointing at what matters, in the language you think in. During a walkthrough the toolbar disappears on purpose, there is one thing to do, listen and interrupt, so that is all the screen offers. The lesson has your full attention because it asks for nothing else.

04

It never advances on a timer

No page ever turns because a video kept playing. Every transition waits for you. Say “slower” and it re-explains at half the pace; say “go back” and the previous page returns with the thread intact. The lesson moves at the speed of your understanding, not the speed of a recording.

05

Interrupt mid-sentence, out loud or typed

Click the mic and talk over it, or type into the sidebar, both work at every moment, and both stop the lesson cold. Your question gets a real answer, drawn on the page when a picture will land better than words. Then “continue lesson” resumes from the exact page where it paused, like the interruption was always part of the plan. Because it was.

06

Practice is one sentence away, and so is the way back

“Let’s practice” swaps in the paired worksheet and unlocks the full toolset: your pen, hints beside the exact problem, Ask Gausyn workspaces. “Switch to teacher mode” brings the lesson back to the page you left. Students move between being taught and trying it themselves as often as the material needs, the way the best tutoring sessions always have.

07

Change course without leaving the room

“Get me a lesson on limits” pulls different material from the bank mid-session. “Switch to homework mode” lets you upload tonight’s actual assignment instead. The session bends to what you need in the moment, no rejoining, no starting over, no losing the conversation you have already had.

08

A real class period, honestly kept

Every session is a clocked lesson with a subject and a countdown on screen, anchored on the server, not a window you can leave open forever. When it ends, the calendar records what actually happened: completed, how long, how much was covered. Parents see a week of real teaching, not a screen-time guess.

The best lectures were always conversations.
