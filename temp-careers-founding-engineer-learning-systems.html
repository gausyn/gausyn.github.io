1.About the role

gausyn has to do more than produce a reasonable answer. When a student brings a worksheet, gausyn reads every page, works out a reference solution for each question, and finds where each question, blank and choice sits, so its hints land in the empty space beside the right one. In a lesson it listens, looks at the board, and replies out loud while it draws, and that has to feel like a conversation, not a wait.

You will own the systems that make that true, and the measurements that show whether it is true. Better teaching is the goal, and a good demo is not the same thing.

2.What you will do

  • Build and harden the pipeline that reads worksheets: text and layout from typed pages, and handwriting and photos where there is no text to read
  • Generate and check reference solutions, so that every hint is grounded in correct working rather than a guess
  • Place hints, steps and drawings on the board beside the right question, and never over what the student wrote
  • Chase latency out of the listen, think, speak and draw loop until a lesson feels like talking to a person
  • Design evaluations that measure teaching quality: whether a hint helped, whether the student got there, and whether the answer was given away
  • Turn real sessions, shared with permission, into test sets and measurable improvements
  • Build what gausyn remembers about each student, so the next lesson starts where the last one ended
  • Build the guardrails: safety checks on files and conversations, and the rules that keep gausyn from doing a student’s work for them
  • Choose the right model for each job by speed, cost and quality, and keep that choice honest as models change
  • Work with the Learning Designer to turn what good teaching looks like into rules gausyn follows

3.What we are looking for

  • Experience shipping speech, vision or language systems to real users
  • Strong programming skills, and comfort working across the server and the browser
  • Experience designing evaluations, and reading the results honestly
  • A habit of measuring quality instead of trusting how an output looks
  • A working understanding of latency: streaming, caching, and where the milliseconds go
  • Comfort with messy real-world input: scanned pages, phone photos, handwriting and background noise
  • A conviction that teaching well is an engineering requirement, not a nice-to-have
  • Able to lead your own work with little guidance in a team that moves fast
  • Writes clearly and concisely about results, including the ones that did not work

4.Nice to have

  • You have built real-time voice or other low-latency systems
  • You have worked on document understanding, handwriting recognition or layout extraction
  • You have built evaluations from scratch for something hard to measure
  • You have worked on mathematical reasoning or step-by-step problem solving
  • You have published research, or carried research into a shipped product
  • You have taught a subject you love
