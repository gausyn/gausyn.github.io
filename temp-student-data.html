Student data

Last updated 19 October 2026gausyn, a product of Inquisara

Student data deserves a plainer promise than a privacy policy usually gives. This page is that promise, written for parents, guardians, teachers and schools. It sits alongside our Privacy Policy, which has the full detail. If the two ever seem to differ, the Privacy Policy is what applies.

gausyn is in private testing and this page describes it as it is today. It will be reviewed with a lawyer, with the Privacy Policy and the Terms, before gausyn opens to the public. If anything here matters to a decision you are making, write to us and we will answer plainly.

1.Who this is for

Everyone who uses gausyn, and the adults around them:

  • students of 13 and over with their own account (13 to 17 with a parent's or guardian's agreement);
  • parents and guardians who make an account for their child, of any school age;
  • teachers and schools looking at gausyn for their students.

gausyn does not offer school accounts yet. Every student uses it through their own account or a parent's. Section 9 says what that means for schools.

2.What we mean by student data

Everything a student gives gausyn or makes in it: the email address and age group on the account; the profile (first name, level or grade, subjects and courses, goals, how they like to be taught, the times they are free, and their time zone); the lessons on their calendar and a one-line summary of each; their Learning conversations; their Projects and the files in them; summaries of material attached ahead of a lesson; the short notes gausyn keeps about how they learn; what they write, draw, type and say in a lesson; their friends and spaces in Collaborate; and how much of their plan's limits they have used.

Section 4 of the Privacy Policy describes each of these in full.

3.Our commitments

  • We do not sell student data. Not to anyone, at any price, ever.
  • No advertising. We do not show advertising in gausyn, we use no advertising cookies or trackers in it, and we do not share student data with advertisers or data brokers.
  • Only what teaching needs. We do not ask a student for a surname, an address, a phone number or a photograph. We ask the age once and keep only the age group.
  • No profiles except for teaching. The notes gausyn keeps are about how a student learns, are there to teach them better, and can be read and deleted in Settings, under Memory. gausyn is built not to keep notes about health, religion, family circumstances or similar private matters.
  • Not used to train AI models. We do not train models on what students write, draw or say, and under the terms we use, the AI companies that run gausyn may not either.
  • It belongs to the student and their family. We hold student data to run gausyn for them, not as an asset of our own. On an account a parent or guardian made for a child, the parent or guardian controls it.
  • We tell you when something goes wrong. If student data is ever exposed, the affected account holders and the relevant regulator hear it from us, as the law requires, with what actually happened.
  • No quiet changes. If we change how student data is handled in a way that matters, we tell account holders directly first, and ask for agreement again where the law requires it.

4.What gausyn keeps, and what it does not

Kept in the account, until you delete it

  • The profile, the calendar, and the one-line summary of each lesson.
  • Learning conversations.
  • Projects: drafts, code, uploaded files and the pictures gausyn made.
  • Summaries of material attached ahead of a lesson.
  • The notes gausyn keeps about how the student learns.
  • Friends and spaces in Collaborate.

Kept only in the student's own browser

  • The whiteboard: the board, its chat, what was written and drawn on it, and the worksheet pages loaded onto it are saved on the device for a short time, so the student can come back to it. We do not store boards on our servers.

Not kept on our servers

  • The conversation from a whiteboard lesson. When the lesson ends it is read once to write the summary and update gausyn's notes, and it is not kept.
  • Voice recordings. They are turned into text and then discarded. Only the text stays, as part of the conversation.
  • Files attached ahead of a lesson. Only their summary is kept.
  • Calls between friends. They are not recorded.
  • Boards shared in a space. They pass through our lesson server to the others in the space and are not stored there.

Kept for a short time by the companies we use

Our AI model provider may keep what it receives for up to 30 days to check for abuse. Our hosting providers keep standard server records, such as internet addresses and error reports, for a limited time.

5.Homework you upload

There are three ways to bring work into gausyn, and each is handled differently on purpose.

  • On the whiteboard, during a lesson. The worksheet or photo is shown on the board. When gausyn answers, a picture of the visible board, with the pages on it, is sent to our AI model provider so gausyn can see the work. The pages are saved with the board in the student's browser, not on our servers.
  • Attached ahead of a lesson, from the daily check-in for example. The file is read once to write a short summary of the topic and the kind of problems, so gausyn knows roughly what is coming. The summary is kept. The file itself is not, which is why gausyn asks for the sheet again when the lesson starts.
  • Uploaded to a Project. The file is stored in the student's account so they can keep working with it, until they delete it or the project.

6.Who processes it

gausyn runs on a small number of companies, each doing one job, and each receives only what its job needs:

  • A database and sign-in provider: sign-in, the database, and stored project files.
  • Hosting providers: hosting for the app and for the whiteboard's lesson server.
  • An AI model provider: gausyn's replies and drawings, and turning speech into text.
  • A voice provider: gausyn's voice. It receives only the words gausyn is about to say.
  • A payment processor: subscriptions, payments and receipts, for families who choose a paid plan.
  • An email delivery service: the emails gausyn sends, such as confirming an address or choosing a new password.

Two smaller things happen in the browser. To show mathematics and to read scanned worksheets, gausyn loads open-source code from public content networks. To connect a call between friends, the browser asks a public connection service for its own internet address. These see the device's internet address, not the student's work.

Nobody else receives student data. Before we add a company that handles student content, we update the Privacy Policy and this page. Anyone can ask us for the current list, with where each company operates.

7.Retention and deletion

We keep student data while the account is open, because a teacher who forgets a student every time is not much of a teacher. The account holder can remove it at any time:

  • Delete one thing: a Learning conversation, a project, a file, or one of gausyn's notes.
  • Start again: Settings, Account, "Reset my learning data" clears lessons, conversations, notes and projects and keeps the account.
  • Delete everything: Settings, Account, "Delete my account" cancels any paid plan and removes the profile, lessons, conversations, notes, projects, files, friends and spaces, and the login itself, straight away.

Our database provider keeps backups for up to 7 days to recover from faults; anything deleted is gone from them once they expire. We keep only what the law requires us to keep, such as payment records, and totals that no longer identify anyone.

A student, parent or guardian can also write to us for a copy of the data, or to have it corrected or deleted. We answer within 30 days, we never charge for it, and we never make gausyn worse because someone asked.

8.Parents and guardians

  • An account for a child is yours. It is under your email address. When you made it, you confirmed that you are 18 or older and the child's parent or legal guardian, and you gave permission for us to collect and use your child's information as the Privacy Policy describes. We keep a record of that and its date.
  • See everything. Sign in: the lessons, Learning conversations, projects and gausyn's notes are all there. Or ask us for a copy.
  • Correct it in Settings, or by writing to us.
  • Delete it, and stop all further collection, with "Delete my account" in Settings, or by writing to us.
  • Friends. Collaborate lets a child add friends by a code and learn with them in a shared space. Please decide together who they add.
  • A teenager's own account. If your child is 13 to 17 and made their own account, you can still ask us at any time to show, correct or delete what we hold.

Before acting on a request about a child, we may check that it really comes from the parent or guardian on the account.

9.Teachers and schools

gausyn does not offer school accounts yet, so today:

  • a school or teacher cannot see a student's gausyn account, and we do not share student data with schools;
  • gausyn receives no records from schools;
  • a teacher may suggest gausyn, but the account belongs to the student or their parent or guardian.

If a school or district wants to use gausyn with its students, please write to us before starting. We would first agree in writing how student data is used and protected, who processes it, how long it is kept and how it is deleted at the end, and nothing would change for students until that agreement is in place. We will also tell you plainly what we do not have yet: an independent security audit, a published accessibility report, and any formal certification.

10.How this fits the law

Inquisara is a Canadian company. It handles personal information under PIPEDA, Canada's federal privacy law, and the provincial laws that apply, such as Quebec's. Where students live elsewhere, we work to the rules that apply there, including:

  • COPPA in the United States, for children under 13: a child cannot make an account, and a parent or guardian makes one and gives permission instead;
  • the student privacy laws of US states, many of which forbid selling student data or using it for advertising, which we never do;
  • the GDPR in Europe and its UK version: the rights to see, correct, take away and delete your data, described in the Privacy Policy.

FERPA, the US law on school records, applies to schools. Without school accounts, gausyn receives no education records from schools.

These pages are working drafts that we are reviewing with a lawyer before gausyn opens to the public. We do not claim certifications or seals we do not hold. If a specific rule matters to you, ask us and we will answer specifically.

11.Security

Traffic is encrypted in transit. Data is stored with access rules so that one account cannot read another's, and our team's access is limited to the people who need it to run and support gausyn. Our servers' logs record sizes and errors, not what students say or write.

No system is perfectly secure, and we will not pretend otherwise. If a breach ever affects student data, we will tell the affected account holders and the relevant regulator as the law requires.
