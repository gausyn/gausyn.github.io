Part one · Teacher mode

A lesson that stops when you say wait.

Join a scheduled session and the lesson is already chosen, already on the board, and taught out loud, page by page, at your pace. Interrupt it mid-sentence and it answers. Say “let’s practice” and the worksheet appears. Say “continue” and the lesson picks up exactly where it paused.

In full

Everything Teacher mode does.

01

Planned by your week, not picked from a menu

Sessions come off your calendar with a subject, grade, and topic already attached, because Gausyn planned them around your real deadlines. An exam on Friday becomes scarcity on Monday and opportunity cost on Thursday. When you join, the right lesson is already loaded. There is nothing to search, nothing to configure, nothing between sitting down and learning.

02

Real lessons, from a bank built by teachers

Lessons are not generated slideshows. They come from a bank of lesson and worksheet pairs written by real teachers, millions of problems across subjects and grades, each lesson matched to your session by subject, grade level, and topic, and each one paired with the practice worksheet written to go with it. Where the bank has no page for your corner of the syllabus, Gausyn generates the material itself, so no topic is ever out of reach.

03

Taught out loud, page by page

Gausyn narrates the lesson the way a teacher works a room: page by page, pointing at what matters, in the language you think in. During a walkthrough the toolbar disappears on purpose, there is one thing to do, listen and interrupt, so that is all the screen offers. The lesson has your full attention because it asks for nothing else.

04

It never advances on a timer

No page ever turns because a video kept playing. Every transition waits for you. Say “slower” and it re-explains at half the pace; say “go back” and the previous page returns with the thread intact. The lesson moves at the speed of your understanding, not the speed of a recording.

05

Interrupt mid-sentence, out loud or typed

Click the mic and talk over it, or type into the sidebar, both work at every moment, and both stop the lesson cold. Your question gets a real answer, drawn on the page when a picture will land better than words. Then “continue lesson” resumes from the exact page where it paused, like the interruption was always part of the plan. Because it was.

06

Practice is one sentence away, and so is the way back

“Let’s practice” swaps in the paired worksheet and unlocks the full toolset: your pen, hints beside the exact problem, Ask Gausyn workspaces. “Switch to teacher mode” brings the lesson back to the page you left. Students move between being taught and trying it themselves as often as the material needs, the way the best tutoring sessions always have.

07

Change course without leaving the room

“Get me a lesson on limits” pulls different material from the bank mid-session. “Switch to homework mode” lets you upload tonight’s actual assignment instead. The session bends to what you need in the moment, no rejoining, no starting over, no losing the conversation you have already had.

08

A real class period, honestly kept

Every session is a clocked lesson with a subject and a countdown on screen, anchored on the server, not a window you can leave open forever. When it ends, the calendar records what actually happened: completed, how long, how much was covered. Parents see a week of real teaching, not a screen-time guess.

Part two · Practice mode

You work. It watches.

Bring a worksheet from the bank, upload tonight’s homework, or ask for questions made just for you. You write on the board in your own hand; Gausyn reads what you wrote, checks it against math it has already worked out, and places help beside the exact problem you are stuck on. Never the answer.

The loop

Hints that land beside the problem, not on top of it.

This is the heart of Practice mode: you write, it watches, and when you stall it asks the one question that gets you moving, placed right next to the problem it belongs to. Every hint is a nudge or a single step, checked against the reference solution Gausyn worked out when the sheet loaded. The moment of actually getting it right is never taken from you.

In full

Everything Practice mode does.

01

Bring anything, or bring nothing

Practice on the worksheet paired with your lesson, pull a different sheet from the bank, “get me a worksheet on trig identities”, upload tonight’s actual homework as a PDF or photos, or ask Gausyn to generate questions from nothing. All four flow through the same board, the same conversation, the same rules of help.

02

A bank written by teachers

The worksheet bank pairs every lesson with practice written to match it, real teachers’ material, matched to your session by subject, grade, and topic, millions of problems deep and growing. This is practice with a point of view, not a random question generator wearing a worksheet costume.

03

It solves the sheet before you start

On upload, Gausyn works every problem to a full reference solution and keeps it private. Your work is checked against real worked math for the rest of the session, which is why it can say “that line is right, keep going” with a straight face, and catch the exact step where things went sideways.

04

It sees your handwriting

Every exchange, Gausyn looks at the board as it actually is, your strokes, your crossed-out attempts, your half-finished line. Help responds to what you actually wrote, not to what a transcript claims you meant. Write it the way you would on paper; it reads it the way a teacher leaning over your shoulder would.

05

Hints, never answers

The help is Socratic by design. A nudge in plain words. One step of math, typeset properly. A question that makes you notice what you missed. Gausyn is graded on whether you could do the next one alone, so it refuses to do this one for you, even when you ask nicely.

06

Three kinds of help, one placement engine

Plain words, a formula step, or a drawn diagram, whichever the moment needs. All three land by the same deterministic rules: stacked beside the question they belong to, out of the way of your ink and the worksheet text, pruning the oldest notes when space runs short. The board stays legible for the whole session.

07

It draws when a picture teaches better

Ask “can you draw this?”, or let it judge that words are failing, and a labelled diagram is sketched beside the problem: the triangle in the word problem, the forces on the block, the shape of the graph. Drag it, resize it, keep it for the rest of the session.

08

Infinite practice that tunes itself

Generated questions arrive one at a time in their own Practice tab, pinned where you cannot lose them. “Harder,” “easier,” “a different one,” “more like word problems”, the next question adjusts instantly, even mid-problem. Breadth when you want variety, depth when you want a fight, mercy when you need a win.

09

Your blanks stay yours

Gausyn never writes in an answer space, not a number, not a letter, not helpfully, not once. It can annotate the margin and circle what you are confirming, but the answer is always in your handwriting. That is the difference between finishing the homework and being changed by it.

10

Boards that remember, and room when you need it

Every board tab keeps its ink, its PDF, its scroll position, and every hint exactly where it was, switch away and back and nothing has moved. And when one problem deserves a full page of thinking, drag a box around it and Ask Gausyn opens a workspace just for it.

Part three · Ask Gausyn

Worksheets give you two inches of margin for a problem that needs a full page of thinking. Ask Gausyn fixes that: drag a box around any problem and it becomes its own workspace, the question pinned at the top, clean grid below, a pin left behind so you can jump straight back.

One gesture

In full

Everything Ask Gausyn does.

01

One gesture, on anything

Click the button, drag a rectangle, done. It works on any problem, on any board, a worksheet from the bank, the homework you uploaded, a generated practice question. If it is on the board and it is hard, it can have a room of its own.

02

The crop is the reference, pinned for good

Your cropped problem, diagram, data table, instructions and all, is pinned at the top of the workspace where it can never scroll away. However deep the working gets, the question is always one glance up, exactly as it appeared on the original page.

03

A clean page for however long it takes

Below the pin is open grid, a full page for the working the worksheet never made space for. Three failed attempts, a diagram, the fourth attempt that lands: it all fits, and none of it crowds the original sheet.

04

The same honest help, inside

In a workspace the rules do not change: Gausyn’s hints stack beneath the pinned reference, plain words, single steps, drawn figures, still grounded against its worked solutions, still never the finished answer, still never touching your blanks back on the sheet.

05

A pin back to the exact spot you left

An anchor stays behind on the source board at the crop. When the side quest is over, one click returns you there, and the workspace stays one tab away if the problem pulls you back in.

06

Rooms that accumulate, and remember

Q1, Q2, Q3, each workspace persists for the whole session with its own ink, hints, and scroll position intact. It is the difference between a whiteboard and a binder: by the end of the hour, your hardest problems each have their own worked page you can walk back through.

What a fraction actually says

  • The bottom number names the size of the pieces.
  • The top number just counts how many you have.
  • Same whole, different cuts, the bar never changes length.

Keeping an equation balanced

  • An equals sign is a promise that both sides weigh the same.
  • Whatever you do to one side, do to the other, or the promise breaks.
  • Undo the last thing that happened to x, first.
3x − 7 = 14
add 7 to both sides
3x = 21
divide both sides by 3
x = 7

Lesson · page 1 of 4

What a thesis has to do

  • It makes a claim someone reasonable could disagree with.
  • It says why, not just what, the because is the argument.
  • If your whole essay could not fail to prove it, it is a summary.
Weak: Macbeth is about ambition.
Arguable: Macbeth’s ambition only becomes fatal once it is witnessed, he kills to be seen as king, not to be one.

What a derivative measures

  • The slope of the curve at a single instant, not over a stretch.
  • Take the average rate, then shrink the interval to nothing.
  • Whatever the average settles on as h vanishes is the derivative.
f′(x) = limh→0 [ f(x+h) − f(x) ] / h

Eigenvectors: the directions that survive

  • A matrix knocks almost every vector off the line it was on.
  • A few directions are only stretched or squashed, never turned.
  • Those are the eigenvectors; the amount of stretch is λ.
