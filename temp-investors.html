The next step in learning.

Individual teaching is the most effective intervention education has ever measured — and the least distributed. We are building the version that reaches everyone.

The problem is rationing.

Every parent already knows the fix for a struggling student: a patient teacher, one on one, week after week. It works so reliably that the wealthy buy it by default. It has simply never scaled — there are not enough great teachers, at not a low enough price, in not enough languages. Everything else the market offers is a compromise with that scarcity: recorded videos that cannot hear the student, fixed curricula tuned to an average child who does not exist, answer apps that finish the homework and end the learning.

What we have built.

Not a chat window — a working teaching loop, live today with our first students. The student says what is coming and a calendar plans the week around their real availability and deadlines. Each session is a clocked lesson on a shared whiteboard where Gausyn can teach a lesson out loud page by page, or watch the student work a worksheet and place help beside the exact question they are stuck on — grounded in solutions it worked out first, and never handing over the answer. The student can interrupt mid-sentence, switch modes by saying so, and ask for harder problems that are written on the spot.

The homepage shows the loop in motion.

Why now.

The interaction this requires — listening, seeing a page, drawing, speaking, all in real time — only recently became fast and cheap enough to feel like a person rather than a form. Meanwhile students have already voted: they bring their homework to general-purpose chatbots built to answer, not to teach. The demand is proven and being served badly. The product that teaches, plans, and remembers the learner wins that seat.

The team.

We are brothers, building in Toronto. Matthew Lisondra is a robotics researcher at the University of Toronto and has taught physics, mathematics, and computer science to hundreds of students; he leads product and engineering. James Lisondra is a medical student at the University of Ottawa with a background in health research and product design; he leads operations, design, and partnerships. We have built together since childhood.

Where we are.

Early, and honest about it: a working product, our first students using it weekly, a growing waitlist, and a long list of things to make better. If backing this at the ground floor is interesting, we would love the conversation: gausyninc@gmail.com.
