Why does the discriminant tell you how many solutions there are?
Ava is thinking
A

Because it is the part of the quadratic formula that sits under the square root:

x = −b ± √b² − 4ac2a

If b² − 4ac > 0, the ± gives two different real roots. If it is exactly 0, both collapse into one. If it is negative, there is no real square root to take, so there are no real roots at all.

Quick check: how many real solutions does x² + 4x + 5 = 0 have?

16 minus 20 is −4, so none?
Ava is thinking
A

Exactly. There is nothing real to take the square root of, so the parabola never reaches the x-axis. Want me to draw it?

Yes, and then give me some to try.
Ava is thinking
Drawing

There it is: the lowest point sits at y = 1, just above the axis. For practice, let’s work some together on the board.

What is a derivative?
A
Pitched for Grade 5

Think about a car’s speedometer. The distance you have driven keeps changing, and the speedometer tells you how fast it is changing right now, at this exact moment. A derivative is a speedometer for anything that changes.

A
Pitched for Grade 11

It is the slope of a curve at one single point. Take two points on the curve, find the slope between them, then slide the second point closer and closer to the first. The slope you settle on is the derivative:

f′(x) = limh→0 f(x + h) − f(x)h
A
Pitched for an undergraduate

The best linear approximation to a function near a point: f(x + h) = f(x) + f′(x) h + o(h). Read it that way and it generalizes cleanly. For a map between vector spaces, the derivative is the linear map given by the Jacobian matrix.

A
Pitched for a doctoral student

For a map between Banach spaces, the Fréchet derivative at x is the bounded linear operator A with ‖f(x + h) − f(x) − Ah‖ = o(‖h‖). Want to compare it with the Gâteaux derivative, where the limit only has to exist along each direction?

Getting Iris…

Quick one: why does salt melt the ice on roads?
Iris is thinking
I

Ice and liquid water are always trading molecules at the surface. At 0 °C the trade is even. Dissolved salt gets in the way of water molecules rejoining the ice, so melting wins, until it is cold enough to balance again. That new balance point is the lowered freezing point:

ΔTf = i Kf m

Rock salt stops being much use somewhere around −10 °C. Want to work out why calcium chloride keeps working when it is colder?

Everything Learning does.

01

A teacher for every subject

Twelve subjects, each with a named teacher and a voice of its own: Ava for mathematics, Kai for physics, Iris for chemistry, Mira for biology, Nolan for computer science, Eliot for English, and more. It is the same voice that follows you onto the whiteboard, so the teacher you talk to tonight is the one who teaches you on Thursday.

02

Frontier intelligence, tuned by people who teach

Every teacher runs on frontier intelligence, then is tuned for its field by people who teach that subject, with the habits a great teacher of it actually has. The physics teacher draws the diagram before the equation. The chemistry teacher never drops a unit. The English teacher sharpens your argument without replacing your voice.

03

Pitched exactly to your level

A Grade 4 student and a doctoral candidate should never get the same answer to the same question. For younger learners Gausyn favors intuition, concrete examples, and short sentences; for advanced ones it teaches at the frontier of the field, with the rigor the subject demands.

04

It knows your week

Your teacher can see what is on your calendar and what came up in your recent whiteboard sessions, and uses it naturally: connecting tonight’s answer to Thursday’s quiz, or to the step that slowed you down last time, without ever reciting your schedule back to you.

05

It remembers what clicked

Learning keeps a record of every concept you have touched and how it went. Open it and the day starts with “pick up where you left off”: what to continue, and what needs another look, with your teacher already aware of exactly where the last conversation ended.

06

Mathematics that reads like mathematics

Answers arrive as they are written, with every formula properly typeset, never as raw symbols. Code comes in real code blocks with the explanation after it. Substantial answers end with one short check-in question, because understanding is something you test, not assume.

07

It draws when you ask

Ask for a picture, a diagram, or “what does it look like” and your teacher draws one: a clean educational diagram in red ink, placed right in the conversation. It never fills the screen with pictures you did not ask for.

08

One tap to the whiteboard

When a question needs practice, worked examples, or a proper quiz, your teacher offers a whiteboard session on exactly that topic. One tap opens the board, preloaded, with your conversation as context, and the session lands on your calendar.

09

The right teacher for every question

Each subject keeps its own conversation. Ask a chemistry question in your mathematics chat and you get a useful pointer, then an offer to continue with Iris, carrying your question across. Questions that merely touch another field, like the algebra inside a physics problem, simply get answered.

10

Bring the page

Attach a PDF or snap a photo of the worksheet, and your teacher reads it with you. Uploads are read in memory for that conversation and never stored on our servers.

11

Talk or type

Turn on voice and talk it through out loud; your teacher answers out loud, in its own voice. Or type in silence at two in the morning. Everything works both ways.

12

Your history, by subject

Every conversation is saved and grouped under its subject, so a month of mathematics sits together, and the essay you were planning in English is exactly where you left it.
