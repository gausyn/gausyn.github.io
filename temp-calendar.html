01

Speak, don't schedule

The calendar has a conversation, not a settings page. “I have a chem exam next Friday” becomes the right number of review sessions, placed inside hours you are actually free, ending early enough to sleep before the exam.

02

Real sessions, not colored blocks

Every lesson runs a lifecycle: Upcoming, Joinable ten minutes early, In progress, then Completed or Missed — with a one-line recap of what happened. The timer lives on the server, so it cannot be gamed from a browser tab.

03

Deadlines that stare back

Exams and due dates sit on the calendar in red with a live countdown. Add them by talking, mark them done when they are beaten. Gausyn plans the week around them, not just up to them.

04

Your availability is law

You set the windows once. Everything — the assistant's plans, your own drags, recurring series — is checked against them. Moving a session somewhere you said you're busy asks first instead of silently obeying.

05

It checks in daily

Once a day, a quick check-in asks what changed: new homework, a moved test, a photo of the worksheet. Upload it and the plan absorbs it — the session that covers it already knows what is coming.

06

Drag anything, still

Everything above is optional. The calendar is a real calendar: drag to move, stretch to resize, click an empty slot to book. It just happens to also listen.

The plan bends. The exam date doesn't.
